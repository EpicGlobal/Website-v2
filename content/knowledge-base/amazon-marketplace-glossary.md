This glossary defines the Amazon, Walmart, TikTok Shop and wholesale terms brand owners run into when they sell through online marketplaces, in one or two plain sentences each. Where it matters, we also say how Epic Global Inc., a wholesale trade partner that buys a brand's inventory and sells it on Amazon first, plus Walmart and other marketplaces, handles that term in practice.

::term[1P (First Party)]

1P means the brand sells its products to Amazon as a supplier, and Amazon resells them to shoppers and controls the retail price. Brands manage a 1P relationship through Vendor Central. Epic itself buys inventory and sells it on the marketplace rather than selling to Amazon as a vendor.

::term[3P (Third Party)]

3P means an independent seller lists products on Amazon's marketplace, sets its own prices and pays Amazon fees on each sale. Sellers manage 3P accounts through Seller Central. Epic buys a brand's inventory at wholesale and sells it at retail as a marketplace seller, paying the fees, advertising and fulfillment itself.

::term[A+ Content]

A+ Content is the enhanced section of an Amazon product page with images, comparison charts and brand copy below the main listing. It is available to brand owners enrolled in Brand Registry (and to vendors) and can lift conversion when done well.

::term[ACoS (Advertising Cost of Sale)]

Ad spend divided by the sales the ads are credited with, shown as a percentage. See Epic's KPI Glossary.

::term[Affiliates and Creators]

Affiliates and creators are publishers, influencers and video creators who promote products and earn a commission on the sales they drive. Common programs include Amazon Associates, Amazon Influencer, and the TikTok Shop affiliate program.

::term[Agency]

An agency is a service firm a brand pays (through retainers, hourly fees or a percentage of sales) to run its marketplace accounts, ads or creative. The brand still owns the inventory, pays the ad spend and fees, and carries the risk if products don't sell. Epic is not an agency. It does not charge retainers or hourly fees; it earns its margin by buying inventory at wholesale and selling it at retail.

::term[Aggregator]

An aggregator is a company that buys Amazon brands outright, taking ownership or equity in the business itself, and runs them as part of a portfolio. The founder sells the company and usually exits. Epic does not buy brands or take equity. The brand stays with its owner; Epic buys the products, not the company.

::term[ASIN (Amazon Standard Identification Number)]

An ASIN is the 10-character ID Amazon assigns to each product in its catalog. Every seller offering the same product shares the same ASIN and competes for the Buy Box on it.

::term[Authorized Seller]

An authorized seller is a reseller the brand has formally approved, usually in writing, to sell its products on a given channel or marketplace. Authorization is what lets a brand tell [unauthorized sellers](/knowledge-base/unauthorized-seller-monitoring-workflow) apart and act against them. Epic operates as an authorized seller for the brands it buys from and works to keep the marketplace limited to authorized sellers, usually as the exclusive authorized seller on the marketplaces and countries named in its agreement with the brand.

::term[Brand Registry]

Amazon Brand Registry is a free program for brand owners with a registered or pending trademark. It gives the brand more control over its listings plus access to tools such as A+ Content, Brand Stores, Sponsored Brands and the brand protection reporting tools. Check Amazon's current eligibility rules before enrolling. The brand keeps ownership of its Brand Registry and authorizes Epic in it, so Epic can manage the listings and act against unauthorized sellers.

::term[Brand Store]

A Brand Store is a free, multi-page storefront on Amazon with its own URL, available to Brand Registry brands. It showcases the full product line and can be the landing page for Sponsored Brands ads.

::term[Buy Box (Featured Offer)]

The Buy Box is the "Add to Cart" and "Buy Now" area on an Amazon product page, and the seller who wins it gets most of that page's sales. Amazon awards it based on factors like price, fulfillment, delivery speed and seller performance. The share of time you hold it is Buy Box %; see Epic's KPI Glossary. Enforcing MAP and removing unauthorized sellers keeps the Buy Box from being won by off-price or gray-market offers.

::term[Consignment]

Consignment is an arrangement where a retailer or seller lists a brand's goods but only pays the brand after they sell; the brand owns the stock until then and carries the risk of unsold inventory. In Epic's standard model, Epic buys the inventory upfront, so the brand is paid for the goods on agreed payment terms rather than waiting on sell-through.

::term[Contribution Margin]

Revenue left after product cost, marketplace fees, fulfillment and advertising; the clearest single measure of whether a product line is profitable. See Epic's KPI Glossary.

::term[Conversion Rate (CVR)]

The share of visits or clicks that become orders. See Epic's KPI Glossary.

::term[CTR (Click-Through Rate)]

Clicks divided by impressions; how often shoppers click when they see your listing or ad. See Epic's KPI Glossary.

::term[Distributor]

A distributor buys inventory from a brand at wholesale and resells it to retailers and other businesses, often across a region or channel. A traditional distributor moves product to other sellers; it usually doesn't run the consumer-facing listings, advertising and brand protection on a marketplace.

::term[Dropshipping]

Dropshipping is a model where the seller lists products it does not hold, and the supplier ships each order directly to the customer. The seller never stocks or pays for inventory in advance. Epic does not dropship. It buys and holds the inventory it sells.

::term[DSP (Amazon Demand-Side Platform)]

Amazon DSP lets advertisers buy display, video and streaming ads programmatically, on and off Amazon, using Amazon's shopper data for targeting. It is typically used for awareness and retargeting rather than search-intent sales.

::term[FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon)]

With FBA, a seller ships inventory to Amazon's warehouses, and Amazon stores, picks, packs, ships and handles returns and customer service for those orders for a fee. FBA offers usually qualify for Prime. Epic pays fulfillment costs itself, so FBA fees are not billed to the brand. Epic uses both FBA and FBM, mostly FBA, and ships FBM orders from its own US warehouse.

::term[FBM (Fulfilled by Merchant)]

With FBM, the seller lists on Amazon but stores and ships orders itself or through its own third-party logistics provider. It gives more control over inventory but the seller must meet Amazon's shipping performance standards.

::term[Gating and Ungating]

Gating is Amazon's restriction on selling certain categories, brands or products without approval. Ungating is getting that approval, often by submitting invoices from an authorized supplier, certifications or a brand's authorization letter. Requirements vary by category, so check Seller Central for the current rules.

::term[Gray Market]

Gray-market goods are genuine products sold through channels the brand did not authorize, often diverted from other regions, retailers or liquidation. They are not counterfeit, but they can undercut pricing, confuse customers and fall outside the brand's warranty or quality control. Epic's brand protection work monitors for unauthorized offers and works to remove them.

::term[GTIN, UPC and EAN]

A GTIN (Global Trade Item Number) is the standard barcode number that identifies a product worldwide; the UPC (12 digits, common in the US) and EAN (13 digits, common in Europe) are types of GTIN. Amazon and Walmart generally require a valid GTIN from GS1 to create a new listing, though exemptions exist in some cases.

::term[Importer of Record (IOR)]

The importer of record is the party legally responsible for bringing goods into a country: filing customs entries, paying duties and taxes, and making sure products meet local rules. On international marketplaces, someone in the chain must take on this role.

::term[IPI (Inventory Performance Index)]

IPI is Amazon's score for how well a seller manages FBA inventory, based on factors like excess stock, sell-through and stranded inventory. A low score can bring storage limits or extra fees. Check Seller Central for the current threshold and how Amazon applies it.

::term[Landed Cost]

Landed cost is the total cost of getting a product to the point of sale: the unit price plus freight, insurance, duties, taxes, customs fees and handling. It is the real cost basis for pricing and margin, especially when selling internationally.

::term[MAP (Minimum Advertised Price)]

MAP is the lowest price a brand allows resellers to advertise for its products, set out in a written MAP policy. It protects brand value and keeps resellers from racing each other to the bottom on price. Epic sells at or above MAP and enforces it as part of its brand protection work, one of the four pillars of its Buy-Sell-Advertise-Protect model.

::term[Marketplace Operator]

A marketplace operator is a company that runs the day-to-day selling on online marketplaces: listings, pricing, advertising, inventory and customer service. The term describes what a company does, not how it is paid, so an operator can be a seller that owns the inventory or a service provider. Epic is a marketplace operator that owns the inventory it sells, on Amazon first, plus Walmart, eBay, TikTok Shop and other marketplaces.

::term[Multichannel Fulfillment (MCF)]

Multichannel fulfillment is using one inventory pool to ship orders from several sales channels. Amazon MCF, for example, lets sellers ship orders from Shopify, Walmart or their own site using inventory stored in FBA. Some marketplaces restrict Amazon-branded packaging or delivery, so check each channel's rules.

::term[Net Terms]

Net terms are payment terms that give a buyer a set number of days to pay an invoice. They are common in wholesale and affect a brand's cash flow. Epic pays for the inventory it buys on payment terms agreed in each supply agreement.

::term[Parent and Child Variations]

A variation family groups versions of one product, such as sizes or colors, on a single Amazon listing. The parent is a non-buyable placeholder; each child is a buyable ASIN with its own price and inventory, and children share reviews.

::term[Private Label]

Private label means a seller has products made by a manufacturer and sells them under its own brand. The seller owns the brand and listing, as opposed to reselling another company's brand.

::term[Project Zero]

Project Zero is an Amazon program that lets eligible brands remove counterfeit listings themselves, without filing a report and waiting for Amazon. It also offers optional unique product serialization. Check Amazon's current eligibility rules.

::term[Removal Order]

A removal order is a request to have Amazon return or dispose of inventory stored in its FBA warehouses. Sellers use them to clear aged, excess or unsellable stock, and Amazon charges a per-unit fee.

::term[Retail Arbitrage]

Retail arbitrage is buying products at a discount from retail stores or clearance sales and reselling them on a marketplace for a profit. These sellers usually aren't authorized by the brand and can disrupt pricing.

::term[ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)]

Ad-attributed sales divided by ad spend; the inverse of [ACoS](/knowledge-base/kpi-glossary). See Epic's KPI Glossary.

::term[Seller Central]

Seller Central is Amazon's portal for third-party (3P) sellers to manage listings, pricing, inventory, orders, advertising and account health. Sellers pay Amazon fees and set their own prices.

::term[Sell-Through Rate]

The share of available inventory sold during a period. See Epic's KPI Glossary.

::term[SFP (Seller Fulfilled Prime)]

Seller Fulfilled Prime lets a seller ship orders from its own warehouse while still showing the Prime badge, as long as it meets Amazon's strict delivery speed and performance standards. Check Amazon for current enrollment status and requirements.

::term[Shopify]

Shopify is a platform brands use to run their own online store. Unlike a marketplace, the store owner controls the customer relationship and data but must bring its own traffic.

::term[Sponsored Brands]

Sponsored Brands are Amazon ads that show a brand's logo, headline and several products (or a video) at the top of and within search results. They require Brand Registry. Epic pays for advertising itself, including Amazon ad campaigns, as part of the Buy-Sell-Advertise-Protect model.

::term[Sponsored Display]

Sponsored Display ads target shoppers by product, category or audience, and appear on and off Amazon, including on competitor product pages. They are often used for retargeting.

::term[Sponsored Products]

Sponsored Products are cost-per-click Amazon ads for individual listings that appear in search results and on product pages. They are usually the largest share of an Amazon ad budget. Epic funds the ad spend; the brand does not pay for clicks.

::term[Stranded Inventory]

Stranded inventory is stock sitting in an Amazon warehouse with no active, buyable listing attached, often because a listing was suppressed or closed. It still incurs storage fees until it is fixed or removed.

::term[TACoS (Total Advertising Cost of Sale)]

Ad spend divided by total sales, ad and organic; shows whether advertising is growing the whole business. See Epic's KPI Glossary.

::term[TikTok Shop]

TikTok Shop is TikTok's in-app marketplace, where shoppers buy directly from videos, live streams and product showcases. Sales are driven heavily by creators and affiliates. TikTok Shop is one of the marketplaces where Epic sells the inventory it buys from brands.

::term[Transparency]

Transparency is Amazon's product serialization program: brands put a unique code on each unit, and Amazon checks the code before shipping so counterfeit units are stopped. Shoppers can also scan the code to verify a product.

::term[Unauthorized Seller]

An unauthorized seller is anyone selling a brand's products without the brand's approval, often below MAP and sometimes with old, damaged or diverted stock. They can take the Buy Box, erode price and hurt reviews. Removing unauthorized sellers is part of Epic's brand protection work.

::term[VAT (Value-Added Tax)]

VAT is a consumption tax charged at each stage of sale in the UK, the EU and many other countries. Selling there usually requires VAT registration and regular filings, and marketplaces may collect VAT on some sales. Rules vary by country and seller location.

::term[Vendor Central]

Vendor Central is Amazon's portal for 1P suppliers that sell wholesale to Amazon. Amazon issues purchase orders, owns the inventory and sets the retail price.

::term[Walmart Marketplace]

Walmart Marketplace lets approved third-party sellers list products on Walmart.com alongside Walmart's own inventory. Sellers apply for approval and pay a referral fee on each sale; check Walmart's current requirements and fees. Walmart is one of the marketplaces where Epic sells the inventory it buys from brands.

::term[Weeks of Cover (WOC)]

How many weeks current inventory will last at the current sales rate. See Epic's KPI Glossary.

::term[WFS (Walmart Fulfillment Services)]

WFS is Walmart's fulfillment program, similar to FBA: sellers send inventory to Walmart warehouses, and Walmart ships orders and handles returns. WFS items can earn fast-shipping badges on Walmart.com.

::term[Wholesale]

Wholesale is selling products in bulk to a business that resells them, at a price below retail. The buyer owns the goods once purchased and takes on the risk of selling them.

::term[Wholesale Trade Partner]

A wholesale trade partner buys a brand's inventory upfront at wholesale and sells it directly to consumers on online marketplaces, paying for the advertising, marketplace fees and fulfillment itself. The brand keeps its company and gets paid for its products, while the partner earns its margin only by selling them at retail. This is Epic's model: Epic buys the inventory upfront and funds the ads, fees and fulfillment.

## How the four models compare

| Model | Who owns the inventory | Who pays ads and fees | How it gets paid | Owns your company? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agency | The brand | The brand | Retainers, hourly or service fees, or a percentage of sales | No |
| Aggregator | The aggregator, after buying the business | The aggregator | Profits of the brands it owns | Yes, it buys the company |
| Distributor | The distributor | Usually the retailers it sells to | Margin on reselling to other businesses | No |
| Wholesale trade partner (Epic) | The partner | The partner | Margin on selling to consumers at retail | No |
