A weekly [operating cadence](/knowledge-base/marketplace-operating-cadence) is a fixed schedule that tells a marketplace team what to review, decide and fix on each day of the week. It turns the work of running Amazon, Walmart and other marketplaces (listings, ads, inventory, pricing and brand protection) into a repeatable loop, so problems are caught within days instead of at month end.

The template below is built for a small team or a single operator. Adjust the days to match when your marketplace reports refresh.

## Why does a fixed cadence matter?

- **Marketplaces change daily.** Prices, competitors, Buy Box ownership, search rank and stock levels all move without warning.
- **Most damage is slow and quiet.** A suppressed listing, a hijacked offer or a SKU drifting toward a stockout rarely triggers an alert on its own.
- **Decisions need data from the same window.** Reviewing ads on Monday and inventory on Thursday with different date ranges leads to conflicting conclusions.

## The template: weekly schedule

| Day | Focus | Tasks | Output | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Data and dashboard | Pull last week's sales, ad, inventory and Buy Box reports for the same 7-day window. Update the weekly executive dashboard. Flag every metric outside target. | Updated dashboard and list of flags | Assign one person |
| Monday | Weekly review meeting (30 to 45 min) | Walk the dashboard. Agree on the top 3 to 5 priorities and owners for the week. | Action list with owners and due dates | Assign one person |
| Tuesday | Advertising | Harvest converting search terms into exact match. Add negatives for wasted spend. Adjust bids by target ACoS and TACoS. Check budgets that ran out early. | Bid and keyword change log | Assign one person |
| Wednesday | Inventory and purchasing | Refresh the reorder model. Place purchase orders for SKUs at or below reorder point. Check inbound shipments and check-in delays. Review aged stock and storage fees. | POs placed, shipment plan, at-risk SKU list | Assign one person |
| Thursday | Catalog and listings | Check for suppressed or inactive listings, changed titles or images, and broken variations. Review new reviews and questions. Run one listing improvement or test. | Catalog issue log, test status | Assign one person |
| Friday | Pricing and brand protection | Check MAP compliance and new unauthorized sellers. File or follow up on enforcement actions. Check price index against key competitors. Plan next week's promotions. | Enforcement log, pricing changes | Assign one person |
| Daily (10 min) | Alerts | Stockouts, Buy Box loss on top SKUs, listing suppressions, account health warnings. | Same-day fix or escalation | Assign one person |

## The template: weekly review meeting agenda

1. **Scorecard (10 min):** total sales, contribution margin, TACoS, conversion rate, Buy Box %, weeks of cover, in-stock rate. Compare with last week, last year and target.
2. **Last week's actions (5 min):** done, not done, and what changed as a result.
3. **Exceptions (15 min):** only the metrics and SKUs flagged on Monday. Ask what caused each one before deciding a fix.
4. **Decisions (5 to 10 min):** purchase orders, price changes, budget shifts, enforcement escalations.
5. **This week's priorities (5 min):** three to five items, each with one owner and a due date.

## The template: monthly and quarterly add-ons

| Cadence | Review | Questions to answer |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | SKU profitability | Which SKUs have negative contribution margin after ads and returns? Fix, reprice or cut? |
| Monthly | Lead times and safety stock | Have actual lead times changed? Are service levels right for each SKU group? |
| Monthly | Ad structure | Are campaigns still organized by intent? Is branded spend defending or cannibalizing organic? |
| Quarterly | Marketplace and region mix | Which marketplaces or countries should get more inventory and ad budget? Any new launches? |
| Quarterly | Seasonal plan | What inventory and promotions are needed for the next peak period, given lead times? |

| Week start date | Example: Buy Box % dropped on top SKU | Example: identify new seller, file report, check MAP | Name | Due date | Open or Done | What changed |

## How do you make the cadence stick?

- **Same day, same time.** Put each block on the calendar as a recurring event.
- **One owner per area.** Shared ownership means nobody checks.
- **Write it down.** Change logs for bids, prices and listings let you connect results to actions.
- **Keep the meeting short.** Discuss exceptions, not every metric that is on track.
- **Review the cadence itself** every quarter and drop steps that never lead to action.

## How Epic handles this

- Epic runs this weekly loop as the Execute stage of its process (Assess, Build, Execute, Scale).
- As a wholesale trade partner, Epic buys the inventory upfront and pays for the ads, fees and fulfillment, so Epic's own team does the weekly work.
- The brand supplies product, data and approvals, and gets a shared dashboard, monthly reports and quarterly reviews.

## FAQ

### How long does the weekly cadence take?

For a small catalog, roughly half a day to a day of focused work per week plus daily alert checks. Larger catalogs need dedicated owners per area.

### Which day should the review meeting be?

Early in the week, once the prior week's data is complete, so decisions can be acted on the same week.

### Does this work for Walmart, eBay and TikTok Shop?

Yes. Keep the same days and add a row per marketplace to the dashboard and action log.

### What should trigger action between weekly reviews?

Stockouts, [Buy Box](/knowledge-base/amazon-marketplace-glossary) loss on top SKUs, suppressed listings and account health warnings. Those go through the daily alert check.
