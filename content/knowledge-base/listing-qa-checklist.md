A listing QA check confirms, before launch, that a new product's title, images, attributes, variations and compliance details are correct and meet marketplace rules. It takes under an hour per listing and prevents the most common launch problems: suppressed listings, wrong search placement, and ad spend sent to a page that does not convert.

Use the checklist below for every new ASIN or Walmart item, and again after any major edit. Category rules differ, so always confirm limits against the current style guide for your category in Seller Central or Walmart Seller Center.

## Why QA a listing before launch?

Launch is when a listing is most fragile. It has no sales history, no reviews and often a large ad budget pointed at it. A missing attribute can keep it out of filtered results. A main image that breaks policy can suppress it. A wrong variation theme can merge it with the wrong products. Fixing these after launch costs ad money and early momentum you do not get back.

## The listing QA checklist

### 1. Identifiers and setup

- GTIN (UPC/EAN) is valid and registered to the brand, or an approved GTIN exemption is in place
- SKU naming follows your internal convention
- Product type and browse category are correct (not just "close enough")
- Brand name matches the Brand Registry entry exactly
- Condition, fulfillment channel (FBA, FBM, WFS) and price are set as planned

### 2. Title

- Within the category character limit. Amazon has tightened title limits: for most non-media categories the cap is now 75 characters (from July 27, 2026). Check the current limit for your category in Seller Central.
- Follows the brand pattern, for example: Brand + product type + key feature + size/count
- No promotional words ("best seller," "sale," "free shipping")
- No word repeated more than twice; no decorative special characters
- Reads clearly on a phone screen where only the first part shows

### 3. Bullets and description

- Each bullet covers one benefit or fact; most important first
- Claims are accurate and supportable (no unapproved health, safety or "guaranteed" claims)
- Dimensions, materials and what is in the box are stated
- No pricing, shipping promises, contact details or links to other sites

### 4. Backend search terms

- Under Amazon's limit (250 bytes in the US)
- Includes synonyms, alternate spellings and use cases not already in the title
- No competitor brand names, no ASINs, no repeated words, no offensive terms

### 5. Attributes

- All required fields filled
- Recommended fields filled where they apply (these drive filters and AI shopping answers)
- Units consistent with the rest of the catalog
- No "N/A" or placeholder values unless allowed

### 6. Images and video

- Main image: product only, pure white background, product fills most of the frame, no text, logos or badges
- At least 1,000 pixels on the longest side so zoom works (Amazon accepts 500 to 10,000)
- Secondary images show scale, use, details, and answer the top buyer questions
- Each variation child has its own correct main image
- Video, if used, is approved and plays

### 7. A+ / brand content

- A+ Content submitted and approved (requires Brand Registry on Amazon)
- No claims in A+ that are not also supported elsewhere
- Comparison chart links to the right sibling ASINs

### 8. Variations

- Child is attached to the right parent
- Variation theme reflects a real difference (size, color, count)
- Child values are named consistently across the family

### 9. Compliance

- Category approval (ungating) in place if required
- Safety documents, labels, Prop 65 warnings, or hazmat (dangerous goods) review completed where they apply
- Packaging and labeling match the listing

### 10. Post-launch checks (day 1 and day 7)

- Listing is active and buyable; not suppressed
- Searching the main keyword in an incognito window finds the product
- Live page matches the approved version (no overwritten fields)
- Ads are pointed at the right ASIN and serving
- Inventory is received and available at the fulfillment center

## What are the most common failures?

| Failure | Typical cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Listing suppressed | Main image or missing required attribute | Check the suppressed listings view, fix the flagged field |
| Not in filtered results | Blank or non-standard attributes | Fill recommended attributes with standard values |
| Wrong category | Product type picked for speed | Correct the product type and browse node |
| Child shows wrong image | Image uploaded to parent only | Upload a main image to each child |
| Live page differs from upload | Another contributor's data or a template error | Compare with source of truth; correct through Brand Registry |

## How Epic handles this

As a wholesale trade partner, Epic buys the inventory upfront and pays for the ads that send traffic to each listing, so a listing that fails QA wastes our money first. Listing QA is done within about 4 weeks of kickoff, before a product goes live with Epic, and the post-launch checks roll into our weekly work.

## FAQ

### How long does listing QA take?

For a single listing with ready content, usually under an hour. [Variation families](/knowledge-base/variation-strategy-basics) and regulated categories take longer.

### Do I need Brand Registry to pass QA?

Not to list, but you need it for A+ Content and for more control over your detail page data on Amazon.

### Should I run QA again after launch?

Yes. Check on day 1 and day 7, then fold the listing into your weekly [catalog hygiene](/knowledge-base/catalog-hygiene-what-it-is-why-it-matters) review.

### Does the same checklist work for Walmart?

Mostly. Walmart has its own required attributes, content scoring and image rules, so check its style guide for the category.
