**Short answer:** in **1P (first party)**, your brand sells to Amazon as a vendor. Amazon buys your stock at a wholesale cost, then decides the retail price and how much to order. In **3P (third party)**, a seller lists your products on Amazon's marketplace through Seller Central and keeps control of price, listings and stock. Many brands earn less per unit in 1P, because Amazon controls the price and vendor terms add allowances and fees.

## What 1P means

In 1P, Amazon is your customer. You sell through Vendor Central, Amazon sends you purchase orders, and Amazon resells your products to shoppers as the retailer.

- **Amazon sets the retail price.** It can lower the price to match other retailers, even below where you want your brand to sit.
- **Amazon decides order volume.** Purchase orders can rise, shrink or pause, and you plan production around a buyer you don't control.
- **Vendor terms add costs.** Co-op, damage and freight allowances, and chargebacks are taken from what Amazon pays you, which makes true margin hard to forecast.

Amazon often prefers to keep large brands in 1P, because 1P gives it control of price and supply.

## What 3P means

In 3P, a seller lists and sells your products on Amazon's marketplace and pays Amazon referral and fulfillment fees.

- **The seller sets the price,** so your price can be held at or above your minimum advertised price (MAP).
- **The seller controls listings, ads and stock,** so content, advertising and inventory are planned together.
- **Fees are clear:** referral fees and Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) fees are published by Amazon.

## 1P vs 3P at a glance

| | 1P (Vendor Central) | 3P (Seller Central) |
|---|---|---|
| Who buys your stock | Amazon | The seller (you, or a wholesale partner) |
| Who sets the retail price | Amazon | The seller |
| Who decides order volume | Amazon | The seller |
| Main costs | Vendor allowances, co-op, chargebacks | Referral fees, fulfillment fees, ads |
| Price protection | Limited | MAP can be held |

## Why brands often earn less in 1P

In 1P, the brand gives up the retail price and absorbs most of the vendor costs, so it keeps a thin share of each sale. In 3P, the price and the stock stay with the seller.

One example: an auto parts brand compared its 1P results with Epic's 3P offer, in which Epic buys the inventory and pays the Amazon fees. Under Epic's offer, the brand's net revenue per unit was projected to be 159% higher on one brake kit and 53% higher on another. These are projections from the offer, not actual sales results.
