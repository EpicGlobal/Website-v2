Search term harvesting means reviewing the Amazon search term report on a fixed schedule, moving terms that convert profitably into their own exact-match targets, and adding terms that spend without converting as negatives. Done weekly, it moves budget toward proven demand and away from waste, which is how most accounts improve efficiency without simply cutting spend.

## What is the search term report?

The Sponsored Products search term report shows the actual words shoppers typed (or the ASINs they were viewing) when your ad was shown and clicked, mapped to the keyword or target that triggered it. Your keyword "protein powder" in broad match might match searches like "vanilla protein powder for women" or "protein shaker." The report shows which of those produced sales and which only cost money.

You can download it from Amazon Ads (Reports or Measurement and Reporting, depending on the console version). Pull it for a window long enough to have meaningful clicks, typically the last 30 to 60 days for discovery campaigns.

## How do you decide what to do with each term?

Set thresholds before you open the report, based on your product's price, margin and conversion rate. A simple decision table:

| What the term shows | Action |
|---|---|
| Orders at or below target ACoS, with repeat conversions | **Promote:** add as exact match in a performance campaign; add negative exact in the source campaign |
| Orders but ACoS above target | **Optimize:** keep, lower the bid, or check the listing for a relevance gap |
| Clicks past your threshold, zero orders, clearly irrelevant | **Negate:** negative exact (or negative phrase if the root word is the problem) |
| Clicks past your threshold, zero orders, relevant | **Investigate:** check price, images and reviews vs. the search results page before cutting |
| Few clicks so far | **Wait:** not enough data yet |
| ASIN (product target) that converts | **Promote:** add to a product targeting campaign |

**Setting the "clicks with no order" threshold:** a common approach is to compare clicks to your typical conversion rate. If your product normally converts at about 10%, 20 or more clicks with no order is a reasonable signal to act. If it converts at 5%, wait longer. Adjust to your own numbers.

## What is the step-by-step harvesting routine?

1. **Download the report** for the discovery campaigns (auto, broad, phrase) and the product targeting campaigns.
2. **Sort by spend.** Start with the terms that cost the most; they matter most.
3. **Tag each term** using the decision table: promote, optimize, negate, investigate, wait.
4. **Promote winners.** Add each to an exact-match ad group in the right performance campaign at a bid near its recent cost per click.
5. **Isolate them.** Add the same term as negative exact in the discovery campaign it came from, so the two campaigns stop competing for the same search.
6. **Negate waste.** Use negative exact for one bad query; negative phrase when a word makes every query irrelevant (for example "used," "replacement part," or a different product type).
7. **Log the changes.** Record date, term, action and reason so you can check results later and undo mistakes.
8. **Review in two to four weeks.** Check that promoted terms hold their conversion rate at the new bid.

## What mistakes should you avoid?

- **Negating too early.** Cutting a relevant term after a handful of clicks throws away future buyers.
- **Using negative phrase too broadly.** Negating "free" may be fine; negating "protein" in a protein account is not.
- **Forgetting to isolate.** Promoting a term without negating it in the source campaign means you bid against yourself.
- **Negating your own brand.** Branded searches belong in brand defense, not on a negative list.
- **Judging by ACoS alone.** A term with a high ACoS but a strong new-to-brand share or high ranking value can be worth keeping for a launch period. Decide on purpose.
- **Ignoring the listing.** If a relevant term never converts, the problem may be the price, the main image or the reviews, not the keyword.

## Weekly harvesting checklist

- Search term report downloaded for discovery and product targeting campaigns
- Thresholds set from current conversion rate and target ACoS
- Winners added as exact match and negated at the source
- Irrelevant terms added as negative exact or phrase
- Converting competitor and complementary ASINs added to product targeting
- Change log updated
- Last cycle's promoted terms reviewed

## FAQ

### How often should I harvest search terms?

Weekly for active accounts. Smaller accounts with low click volume can do it every two weeks.

### Should I remove a keyword from the auto campaign after harvesting it?

You cannot remove a term from an auto campaign, but you can add it as a negative exact so the exact-match campaign handles it.

### What is the difference between negative exact and negative phrase?

Negative exact blocks one specific search. Negative phrase blocks any search containing that phrase, so use it carefully.

### Does this work on Walmart?

Yes. Walmart Connect provides search term data and negative keywords; the menus and match options differ slightly.
