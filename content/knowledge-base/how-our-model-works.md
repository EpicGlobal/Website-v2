## How Epic's Wholesale Trade Partner Model Works

Epic Global Inc. is an Amazon-first wholesale trade partner. We buy a brand's inventory at a negotiated wholesale price, then run the marketplace business in four parts: Buy, Sell, Advertise, Protect. We sell on Amazon first, plus Walmart and international marketplaces, pay the advertising, marketplace fees and fulfillment for the stock we own, and sell at or above your MAP.

Our model works because we own the inventory we buy, so the cost and risk of selling it at retail sit with us. The brand keeps its business, trademarks and Brand Registry, and is paid on the wholesale terms in its contract.

Most marketplace models work the other way: the brand funds the inventory, fees and ads, and carries the downside whether performance improves or not. Growth should not require brands to gamble capital or coordinate a patchwork of agencies and vendors who each optimize only their slice of the problem. The work, the risk and the incentives are interconnected.

Epic uses the same operating framework in every marketplace and region where we sell. Whether you need stronger U.S. results or are planning international expansion, you get one partner that owns the inventory and runs the work.

## The Problem: Brands Carry All the Downside

Many brands assume their hardest problems begin internationally. In reality, the same structural issues are already punishing them at home.

In the U.S., brands face rising fulfillment fees, advertising inefficiencies, pricing erosion, and intense competition for branded search demand. It's common to see revenue grow while profitability shrinks, because costs scale faster than performance.

Even brands with strong products and loyal customers can find themselves trapped: they can't stop spending, but they also can't confidently predict what growth will cost next quarter.

International expansion compounds the same problems. Inventory risk increases. Operational complexity multiplies. Marketplaces operate differently. Timelines stretch. The brand is asked to front even more capital, manage even more moving parts, and accept even more uncertainty.

Across both domestic and international environments, the traditional expectation is the same: the brand funds the bet and absorbs the loss if it doesn't work.

That structure creates a second problem: fragmented accountability. One partner touches listings. Another touches ads. Another touches fulfillment. Another handles "brand protection" — sometimes only when something goes wrong. Each vendor can report success within their scope while overall profitability stalls. Growth can look positive on the surface and still be fragile underneath.

## The Operating Model: Buy – Sell – Advertise – Protect

Epic operates through a unified framework: Buy – Sell – Advertise – Protect. This governs both U.S. execution and international expansion. It is the reason the model works — not as theory, but as a system.

:::card
### Buy: Outright inventory purchase

Epic purchases inventory outright at negotiated wholesale prices. For the brand, this turns inventory into a wholesale sale, paid on agreed payment terms, and moves the inventory risk to Epic. Instead of tying up capital in uncertain outcomes, the brand gets predictable economics at the start of the cycle.
:::

:::card
### Sell: End-to-end marketplace operation

Epic runs its listings and offers across marketplaces on one operating cadence: pricing at or above your MAP, listing optimization, inventory allocation and fulfillment setup, following your approvals and each marketplace's rules.
:::

:::card
### Advertise: Epic funds the spend, then optimizes for profitability

Epic funds and manages advertising across marketplaces. That matters because it changes the incentive structure. When a brand funds ad spend, the system often defaults to "spend to grow," even when efficiency deteriorates. When Epic carries the spend, campaigns must earn their keep. Optimization is tied to profit outcomes, not budget utilization.
:::

:::card
### Protect: Brand equity is defended while growth is built

Marketplace growth brings exposure: unauthorized sellers, counterfeits, catalog changes and competitors bidding on your brand name. Epic builds protection into daily operations. Through the Brand Protection Command Center™, we sell at or above your MAP, document violations and report them through your Brand Registry, and watch branded search and your catalog in every region we serve.
:::

*This is the core difference between "growth services" and a true commerce operator. Services improve parts of the machine. Operators control the machine.*

## The Financial Reality: 1P vs Epic's 3P Structure

For many brands, the U.S. marketplace problem isn't demand. It's economics.

The 1P vs 3P Financial Breakdown case study highlights why. In Amazon Vendor Central (1P), Amazon buys the inventory and sells it to shoppers. Vendor agreements often include allowances, chargebacks and other deductions that cut what the brand actually keeps, and they vary from agreement to agreement.

In the Auto Parts Brand comparison, Epic offered a 3P deal with an agreed purchase price, with Epic paying the marketplace fees. Under that offer, the brand's projected net revenue per unit was higher than under Amazon 1P. This is a projection from the offer, not an actual result after switching.

That distinction matters most in the U.S., where fulfillment fees, advertising costs, chargebacks, and operational penalties can quietly erode profitability. With a negotiated wholesale price, brands know what they earn per unit sold to Epic, and Epic pays the retail selling costs on the stock it buys. Total revenue still depends on how much Epic buys and the contract terms.
