A weekly executive dashboard should fit on one page and answer five questions: are we growing, are we making money, is advertising pulling its weight, will we stay in stock, and is the brand protected. That means about 12 to 15 metrics, each shown against last week, the same week last year and target, plus a short list of decisions needed.

Most dashboards fail by showing too much. An executive needs the few numbers that change a decision this week, with the detail one click away.

## What sections should the dashboard have?

1. **Sales:** total sales, units, average selling price, and the split by marketplace.
2. **Profit:** contribution margin in dollars and as a percentage. This is the most important line on the page.
3. **Advertising:** ad spend, TACoS and ACoS, and organic sales %.
4. **Conversion:** sessions, conversion rate and Buy Box %.
5. **Inventory:** weeks of cover, in-stock rate, inbound units and SKUs at risk of stockout.
6. **Brand protection:** unauthorized sellers found and removed, MAP violations open.
7. **Decisions and actions:** three to five items that need an owner or approval this week.

Definitions and formulas for every metric are in The Marketplace KPI Glossary.

## How should each metric be shown?

- **Always with comparisons.** A number alone means nothing. Show last week, the same week last year (for seasonality) and the target.
- **With a status, not a color only.** Use words such as "On track", "Watch" and "Act" so the page reads in black and white too.
- **In dollars and percentages for profit.** Margin percentage can rise while margin dollars fall.
- **With a 4 to 13 week trend line** for the top metrics, since one week is noisy.
- **With a drill-down** to marketplace and top 10 SKUs, so the "why" is easy to find.

## What should be left off?

- Keyword-level ad data. That belongs in the advertising review.
- Vanity totals such as impressions with no link to sales.
- Metrics nobody owns. If a number has no owner and no action, remove it.
- Blended averages without a SKU view. An account average can hide a few SKUs losing money.

## How do you build one?

1. Pull marketplace sales, ad and inventory reports on a fixed day each week, for the same seven-day window every time.
2. Join them by SKU and marketplace in one sheet or BI tool.
3. Calculate contribution margin using actual fees, fulfillment, ads and an estimate for returns.
4. Set targets for each metric, then add status rules (for example "Watch" if more than 5% off target).
5. Write the notes block and decisions list before the meeting, not during it.

## FAQ

### Why weekly and not daily?

Daily numbers are noisy and invite overreaction. Weekly is fast enough to catch problems and slow enough to show real trends. Daily alerts can still cover stockouts and [Buy Box](/knowledge-base/amazon-marketplace-glossary) loss.

### What is the single most important metric?

Contribution margin, because it shows whether sales growth is profitable after ads, fees, fulfillment and returns.

### Should the dashboard cover every marketplace?

Yes, with a total line and a row per marketplace, so a problem in one channel is not hidden by growth in another.

### How many metrics is too many?

If it does not fit on one page, it is too many. Around 12 to 15 is a practical limit.
