The Ad Account Rebuild Checklist is a step-by-step plan for replacing a disorganized marketplace advertising account with a clean [campaign structure](/knowledge-base/campaign-structure-playbook), without losing the sales and keyword data the old account built up.

Use it when the account has too many overlapping campaigns to manage, spend is rising faster than sales, nobody can explain why a campaign exists, or a new team is taking over.

The examples use Amazon Ads terms (Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display). The same steps apply to Walmart Connect and other marketplace ad platforms.

## When does an account need a rebuild instead of a tune-up?

- The same keyword is targeted in several campaigns, so you bid against yourself.
- Campaigns mix many unrelated products, so you cannot see which product a sale came from.
- There are no consistent naming conventions, portfolios or negative keyword lists.
- Budgets run out early in the day on some campaigns while others barely spend.
- Total advertising cost of sales (TACoS) is rising while organic sales are flat.

If only one or two of these apply, fix them in place. If most apply, rebuild.

## How do I rebuild without losing sales?

Never pause the old account all at once. Old campaigns hold keyword history and steady sales. Build the new structure alongside, move proven keywords across, add negatives to the old campaigns so the two do not compete, and wind the old campaigns down only after the new ones are carrying the volume.

## The checklist

### Phase 1: Audit (before changing anything)

- Export at least 60 to 90 days of campaign, ad group, targeting and search term reports, if available.
- Record baseline numbers: ad spend, ad sales, ACoS, TACoS, conversion rate, total sales, by product.
- List every search term that produced orders, with its spend, orders and ACoS.
- List every search term that spent money with zero orders over a meaningful number of clicks.
- Find duplicate targeting: the same keyword and match type in more than one campaign.
- Note bidding strategies and placement adjustments on each campaign.
- Check listing health on advertised products. Ads cannot fix a suppressed, out-of-stock or weak listing (run the Listing QA Checklist first).
- Agree goals by product: launch, grow, defend or profit. Each needs a different target ACoS.

### Phase 2: Design the new structure

- Create portfolios by brand, product line or goal so budgets can be managed in groups.
- Write a naming convention and use it everywhere, for example: Marketplace | Ad type | Product or group | Targeting | Match type | Goal.
- Separate campaigns by purpose, using the table below.
- Group only closely related products (same product, its variations) in one ad group.
- Build a shared negative keyword list (irrelevant terms, wrong product types).
- Pick a bidding strategy per campaign: dynamic bids down only is the usual cautious start; up and down for proven exact-match winners.
- Set budgets by goal, not evenly.

| Campaign type | Purpose | Targeting |
|---|---|---|
| Brand defense | Hold your own brand searches and product pages | Exact brand terms; your own ASINs |
| Core performance | Scale proven converting terms | Exact match on harvested winners |
| Discovery | Find new converting search terms | Auto, broad and phrase match |
| Product targeting | Show up on related and competitor product pages | ASIN and category targets |
| Upper funnel (later) | Brand awareness and remarketing | Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display |

### Phase 3: Migrate in stages

- Launch brand defense and core performance campaigns first, using the proven search terms from the audit.
- Start new bids near the old effective cost per click, not far above it.
- Add each migrated keyword as a negative exact in the old campaign that held it, so the two do not compete.
- Launch discovery campaigns with negatives for every term already in core campaigns.
- Reduce old campaign budgets step by step as new campaigns pick up volume.
- Archive (do not delete) old campaigns once their spend is near zero, to keep history.
- Log every change with a date so you can link results to actions.

### Phase 4: Stabilize and test

- Leave new campaigns mostly alone for the first one to two weeks while data builds. Amazon's attribution window means recent sales keep updating for several days.
- Weekly: harvest converting search terms from discovery into core exact match.
- Weekly: add negatives for search terms with spend and no orders.
- Weekly: adjust bids toward each campaign's target ACoS.
- Test one variable at a time (bid strategy, placement adjustment, new ad format) and record the result.
- Compare against the Phase 1 baseline after four to eight weeks, looking at TACoS and total sales, not ad sales alone.

## How Epic handles this

Epic is a wholesale trade partner, not a paid ad manager. It buys the brand's inventory upfront and pays for the advertising itself, so every wasted ad dollar comes out of Epic's own margin. There are no retainers, hourly fees or percentage-of-spend charges.

## FAQ

### Will a rebuild cause a drop in sales?

It can if old campaigns are paused before new ones carry the volume. A staged migration, with negatives added to old campaigns as keywords move, limits that risk.

### How long does an ad account rebuild take?

Audit and design usually fit in one to two weeks; migration and stabilization take several more weeks. Judge results after four to eight weeks of clean data.

### Should I delete old campaigns?

No. Archive them. Archived campaigns keep their reporting history, which helps when you compare results later.

### What is the difference between ACoS and TACoS?

[ACoS](/knowledge-base/kpi-glossary) is ad spend divided by ad-attributed sales. TACoS is ad spend divided by total sales, including organic. TACoS shows whether advertising is lifting the whole business.
