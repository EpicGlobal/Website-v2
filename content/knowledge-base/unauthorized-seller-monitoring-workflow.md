To deal with unauthorized sellers, monitor your listings for new offers, identify who each seller is, collect evidence of a real violation, and then use the right tool for that violation: a marketplace infringement report, a test buy, or a direct notice to the seller.

It is important to know the limit up front: in the US, reselling genuine, unaltered goods is generally lawful, so a seller being "unauthorized" is not by itself grounds for Amazon to remove them.

Effective enforcement is evidence-based. It targets sellers who are breaking a law or a marketplace policy, and it reduces leaks in the supply chain so fewer unauthorized sellers appear in the first place.

## What can and can't enforcement do?

| Situation | Can it usually be removed through Amazon? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Counterfeit product | Yes, with evidence | Trademark infringement; counterfeit report through Brand Registry |
| Listing uses your copyrighted images or text without permission on a separate page | Often yes | Copyright report |
| Product infringes a patent | Possibly | Patent report or Amazon's patent evaluation process |
| Goods materially different from genuine (missing warranty, altered, expired, wrong region, damaged packaging) | Sometimes | Material-difference argument; needs strong evidence and often legal support |
| Genuine, new, unaltered goods sold below MAP | No, not through Amazon | Amazon does not enforce MAP; handle through your own reseller and MAP policies |
| Genuine goods sold by a seller you never authorized | Usually no | First sale doctrine generally allows resale of genuine goods |

Filing reports that are not supported by evidence is risky. Amazon can restrict a brand's access to reporting tools for inaccurate or abusive reports.

## What is the monitoring workflow?

### Step 1: Monitor

- Check the offer listing ("Other sellers on Amazon") on every active ASIN; daily for top sellers, weekly for the rest
- Track who holds the Buy Box and at what price
- Watch for new ASINs using your brand name, and for edits to your detail pages
- Check other marketplaces where your brand sells (Walmart, eBay)

### Step 2: Identify

- Record the seller name, seller ID, storefront link and business address shown on the seller profile
- Note fulfillment method (FBA or merchant-fulfilled), price, and claimed condition
- Match against your authorized reseller list and distributor records

### Step 3: Collect evidence

- Timestamped screenshots of the offer, price and seller profile, with the URL visible
- A test buy for suspected counterfeit or materially different goods: order ID, photos of packaging, labels, lot codes and serial numbers, compared to your genuine product
- Any lot or batch codes that trace the goods to a specific distributor or retailer

### Step 4: Classify

Put each case into one category from the table above. The category decides the action. If you can't prove a violation, the case goes to supply chain follow-up, not a takedown.

### Step 5: Act

- **Counterfeit or IP violation:** file through Amazon Brand Registry's Report a Violation tool with the evidence. Brands enrolled in Project Zero may have extra self-service removal tools, if eligible.
- **Materially different goods:** document the differences carefully; consider counsel before sending notices.
- **MAP breach by an authorized reseller:** enforce under your MAP or reseller policy (warnings, then the consequences stated in that policy, such as stopping supply).
- **Genuine goods from an unknown source:** trace the lot codes, find the leak in your distribution, and tighten agreements.

### Step 6: Follow up

- Recheck the listing after any report or notice
- Log the outcome and date
- Escalate repeat offenders according to your escalation plan

## How do you prevent unauthorized sellers in the first place?

- **Written reseller and MAP policies.** Clear terms for who may sell where, and at what advertised price.
- **Distribution agreements.** Limit online resale rights and require distributors to pass terms down.
- **Lot and serial tracking.** Codes on packaging let you trace leaked stock to its source.
- **Brand Registry and, where available, Transparency.** Transparency adds unique codes to each unit so Amazon can check authenticity before shipping.
- **Fewer channels.** The more distributors you have, the more places stock can leak from.

## FAQ

### Can Amazon remove a seller just because I didn't authorize them?

Generally no. Amazon acts on intellectual property violations and policy breaches, not on whether a seller is on your authorized list.

### Does Amazon enforce MAP?

No. MAP is a policy between a brand and its resellers. Amazon sets its own prices and does not police MAP for brands.

### What is a test buy?

Ordering the product from the suspect seller to inspect it. It is the strongest evidence for counterfeit or materially different goods.

### Is this legal advice?

No. Laws differ by country and situation. Talk to an IP attorney before sending legal notices.
